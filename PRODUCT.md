# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Liflow serves individuals who want to capture and revisit daily life without an account, cloud dependency, advertising, or tracking. They use an Android phone throughout the day for quick notes, voice capture, tasks, project updates, and quiet review, including while offline.

## Product Purpose

Liflow is a local-first personal life record. It makes capture fast, organization light, and reflection calm while keeping SQLite data and readable Markdown under the user's control. Success means a user can record something with little friction, find it later, understand where it came from, and recover it without relying on a remote service.

## Brand Personality

Fast, lightweight, gentle. The interface should feel calm and trustworthy without becoming decorative, precious, or vague.

## Anti-references

- Marketing-style layouts that explain the product instead of opening directly into the task.
- Analytics dashboards, oversized metrics, and decorative card grids that make personal records feel operational or gamified.
- Cloud-first patterns, account prompts, social mechanics, advertising, or language implying that private content leaves the device.
- Dense enterprise controls, hidden icon-only actions without semantics, and visual novelty that slows repeated use.

## Design Principles

- Open into the user's current task and keep common actions within one clear gesture.
- Preserve data provenance: search, restore, and navigation must lead back to the exact source.
- Keep technical infrastructure invisible while making user-relevant states understandable.
- Prefer quiet, familiar controls and restrained hierarchy over decorative containers.
- Treat offline operation, recovery, and user-owned files as core product behavior.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Support light and dark themes, 320dp-wide screens, at least 1.3x text scaling, Chinese input methods, and Android back gestures without overflow or occlusion. Icon buttons require semantic labels and at least 48dp touch targets. State and selection must not rely on color alone, and motion should remain brief and functional.
